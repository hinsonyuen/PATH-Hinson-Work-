# Raw scans of China's official trade returns, 1859–1914

The Chinese equivalent of the Ōkurashō *Annual Return of the Foreign Trade of the Empire of Japan* scans: the annual trade returns published by the Chinese Maritime Customs Service, the Qing (later Republican) government's own trade statistics office. All of these are page-image scans on HathiTrust, like the Japan volumes.

**Layout to expect (to confirm when you open the volumes):** through the 1880s and 1890s each year has an abstract of foreign trade for all China (Part I) and returns and reports for each treaty port (Part II). From about 1904 the returns are split into Part I (report and abstract), Part II (port trade statistics and reports) and **Part III (*Analysis of Foreign Trade*: imports and exports by commodity and by country)**. Part III is the nearest match to the commodity-by-country tables you used for Japan.

## Things to watch for when extracting arms data

- **Government arms are probably missing.** Under the 1858 tariff rules, munitions could be imported only by the government or under permit, usually duty-free. Check each year's report for a note on "Government stores" or "munitions of war". It may give a total value even if the commodity tables leave them out.
- **Find the category name first.** In the Japanese returns the line was "Arms & munition of war". The Customs names will differ (look under arms, ammunition, gunpowder, saltpetre, and military stores) and may change with the 1902 tariff revision. The 1903/07–1913/17 index should help for the later years.
- **"Hong Kong" as a country of origin** stands in for many real origins in the by-country tables, especially before Part III starts in about 1905.
- **Currency:** values are in Haikwan taels. Each volume gives the sterling and dollar exchange rate for the year, which you'll need to compare with Japan in yen.
- **English vs Chinese editions:** for 1903–1912 you can use the Chinese edition if you want the Chinese-language original. Check that the tables match the English version before choosing one as your citation.
