## 2. 1868–1881: official Japanese statistics exist, but in a different form

**Before you commit to 1868–81:**
- The comparison tables probably give national totals by commodity but **not by country**. That means 1868–81 rows may not match the country columns in your workbooks. Open the Meiji 1–27 volume and check whether arms, gunpowder and so on are listed separately.
- I haven't confirmed the currency units in these early tables. Yen became the unit of account in 1871, but some early returns are in Mexican dollars. Check each volume's header before combining.

## 3. 1860–1867: no Japanese government statistics

Official trade statistics begin in 1869. For 1860–67 (and as a cross-check for 1868–81), the standard source is the British consular reports on Yokohama, Nagasaki and Hakodate. They are printed in the UK *Parliamentary Papers* and reprinted in this series on HathiTrust (all full view):

*Reports from Her Majesty's consuls on the manufactures, commerce, &c. of their consular districts*: https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/000544288

The record lists volumes for every year up to 1886. The Japan-only *Commercial reports from Her Majesty's Consuls in Japan* (https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/102684739) starts only in 1883.

**Caveats for this period:**
- **1860–61:** I found no published returns. The general consular-report volumes on HathiTrust start with 1862–63, so these two years may only exist in Foreign Office archives (FO 46 / FO 262 at the UK National Archives).
- **Coverage:** these are British consuls reporting on their own ports. Arms often come in a single "arms and ammunition" line, if listed at all. Smuggled arms, and the large arms sales of 1863–68 to domains such as Satsuma and Chōshū, are known to be under-recorded.
- **Not like-for-like:** these are figures by port, in dollars, gathered by a different compiler. They don't fit the same columns as the 1882+ returns, so you'd want to keep them as a separate series or sheet.

## Questions to settle before extracting

1. Do you want pre-1882 data as a **separate series** with a methods note (my recommendation), or merged into the same sheets?
2. Is a 1868–81 dataset of **national totals only** useful for your argument, or do you need trading partners?
3. Does your research question need the Bakumatsu years (1860–67)? The consular figures are the weakest in the set.

Further reading on how reliable these early statistics are: the chapter "Statistics of Tokugawa Coastal Trade and Bakumatsu and Early Meiji Foreign Trade, Part 2" (in *Early Japanese Trade, Administration and Interactions with the West*; JSTOR: https://www.jstor.org/stable/25791342).
